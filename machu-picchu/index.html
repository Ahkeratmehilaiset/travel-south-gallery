Machu Picchu

← SacsayhuamánAll destinationsOllantaytambo →

The Spanish never found Machu Picchu, yet local farmers were living there when Hiram Bingham arrived in 1911.

High on a ridge above the Urubamba, Machu Picchu was built as a royal estate, most likely for the Inca ruler Pachacuti. AMS radiocarbon dating published in 2021 shows the site was in use by about AD 1420 (Burger et al. 2021). Its granite walls lock together without mortar, and near the June solstice a play of light can be seen in the curved Torreón (Kościuk & Ziółkowski 2020): a light effect around the solstice, not a precise calendar.

But... researchers estimate the finest stonework is Inca; some enthusiasts, such as Brien Foerster, ask whether some of it belongs to an older culture, a view mainstream archaeology rejects.

From Inca times: sacred rocks and mountains

T In the Cusco region the high mountains are called apus. They were, and still are, considered the most powerful deities, and Machu Picchu lies at the centre of such a sacred landscape (Reinhard 2007).

T Salcantay (6,271 m), which rises south of Machu Picchu, was highly revered in Inca times. The priest Cristóbal de Albornoz wrote in 1583 that the mountain was very revered (Reinhard 2007).

L The chronicler Juan de Santa Cruz Pachacuti wrote that the god Tonopa, or Tunupa, banished the huacas to mountains, among them Salcantay. This is the same Tunupa whom tradition sees in the Pinkuylluna rock at Ollantaytambo (Reinhard 2007).

T According to Reinhard, Huayna Picchu, north of Machu Picchu, was itself a sacred mountain, with ceremonial carvings in boulders on and near its summit (Reinhard 2007).
