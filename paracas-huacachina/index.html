Paracas & Huacachina

Why would a culture bind the heads of its babies so tightly that their skulls grew long?

The Paracas peninsula, where desert meets a cold, wildlife-rich sea, gives its name to the Paracas culture, about 800 BC to AD 100, famous for some of the finest textiles of the ancient world. In the 1920s Julio Tello uncovered its necropolis, including elongated skulls, which scientists explain by deliberate head binding in infancy. Inland, the Huacachina oasis is ringed by towering dunes.

But... researchers estimate the shaping was a cultural marker of status or identity, while some enthusiasts ask whether DNA points to an unknown lineage; those claims have not appeared in peer-reviewed research.
