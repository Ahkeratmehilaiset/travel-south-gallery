Ollantaytambo

Huge pink stones lie abandoned on the road from a quarry on the other side of the river.

Ollantaytambo is one of the few Inca towns still lived in on its original street grid. Above it rises a temple-fortress built mainly under Pachacuti and left unfinished when the Spanish arrived. Its Wall of the Six Monoliths is made of pink rhyolite hauled from the Kachiqhata quarry across the Urubamba; archaeologist Jean-Pierre Protzen studied the ramps and tools used.

But... researchers estimate the rough and refined stonework belong to Inca phases; some enthusiasts ask whether they are separate, much older building phases.

Why carry stones across a river when there is rock on this side?
