Pisac & the Sacred Valley

The cliff above Pisac is honeycombed with tombs, making it one of the largest known Inca cemeteries.

Pisac's terraces sweep down the mountainside like a giant staircase above the Sacred Valley. The site, generally dated to Pachacuti's reign in the 15th century, combines farming terraces, fortified sectors and a ritual centre of finely fitted stone with its own Intihuatana. Below, the colonial town is known for its large market.

But... was Pisac a royal estate, a fortress or a sanctuary? Researchers estimate it may have been all three; some enthusiasts ask what the Intihuatana stones were really used for.

Which would you choose: the ruins at dawn or the market at noon?
