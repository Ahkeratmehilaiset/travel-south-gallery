El Fuerte de Samaipata

← Tiwanaku & Puma PunkuAll destinationsSalar de Uyuni →

A whole sandstone ridge, about 220 metres long, carved into channels, seats, niches and animal figures.

El Fuerte de Samaipata in eastern Bolivia is attributed to pre-Inca peoples, with later Inca buildings around the rock, and was listed by UNESCO in 1998. Felines and serpents are among the carvings, and erosion now threatens them, so visitors may no longer walk on the rock.

But... the two long parallel grooves along the top spark the most speculation. Researchers estimate they were ritual water channels; some enthusiasts ask whether they were a launch ramp, an idea with no archaeological support.

El Fuerte: grooves, serpent and felines

T The roughly 220-metre red sandstone rock of El Fuerte is carved with animals, geometric shapes, niches and channels. The most famous are two long parallel grooves with smaller zigzag channels between and beside them; the local name is El Dorso de la Serpiente, “the serpent's back” (UNESCO 883; Wikipedia: El Fuerte de Samaipata).

T The site was a ritual and residential area as early as about AD 300, under the Chané of the Mojocoya period; later the Incas built an administrative centre there, and according to UNESCO an Inca-period wall cuts across some carvings, showing they are older than the Incas (Wikipedia: El Fuerte de Samaipata; UNESCO 883).

S Jani's note: the site is famous because the grooves look as if an ancient tank had driven across the rock and left its tracks. The comparison is a popular description, not a research finding; researchers regard the grooves as ritual water channels.

T The western part of the rock has two felines carved in high relief, the only high reliefs at the site, and further east a probable feline head (UNESCO 883). Archaeologists who studied the erosion consider one figure possibly a jaguar and the identification of another puma uncertain; there was also a crawling serpent (Architectus 2020). L Jani also mentions a condor; old descriptions mention a large bird cut in outline, but do not name the species (heritage.world).

L Jani's note: the rock has three large faces that were originally covered in gold. Jani photographed the cliff from the car on the way to El Fuerte on 12 August 2011; the photos are among the pictures on this page. We found no mention of three faces or of gilding in written sources. In 1832 Alcide d'Orbigny interpreted the carvings as a gold-washing plant, but that reading has since been rejected (heritage.world).
