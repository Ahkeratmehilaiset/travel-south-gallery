El Fuerte de Samaipata

A whole sandstone ridge, about 220 metres long, carved into channels, seats, niches and animal figures.

El Fuerte de Samaipata in eastern Bolivia is attributed to pre-Inca peoples, with later Inca buildings around the rock, and was listed by UNESCO in 1998. Felines and serpents are among the carvings, and erosion now threatens them, so visitors may no longer walk on the rock.

But... the two long parallel grooves along the top spark the most speculation. Researchers estimate they were ritual water channels; some enthusiasts ask whether they were a launch ramp, an idea with no archaeological support.
