What do the skulls tell us?

On the side of the skull, MRI DB is written in ink. Could a few letters tell us where this skull came from and where its records are? This page follows the clue as far as the opened sources go.

How the material came together

“I have travelled all over the globe, and here I am holding the Starchild 2 skull in my hands.”

Jani Korpi on photo 04, message of 20 November 2026 (wording smoothed, translated from Finnish)
  1. Peru, 1 November 2014. Jani photographed elongated skulls in Peruvian museums and in a private collection. Photos 01 and 03–11 are photographs from this visit; in photos 03 and 04 Jani himself is holding the skull. In the Travel South videos the owner of the collection talks about his skulls, Starchild 2 is presented by name, and filming in the back room of one museum was specially permitted. Photo 02 is a compilation of these videos.
  2. Cairo, 15 February 2024. In the Egyptian Museum he came across stone heads whose crown extends far back in the same way.
  3. Archive 2026. The photos, videos and open sources were reviewed: museum guides, object records and studies.

Three questions

  1. Where does the code on the skull lead? The ink-written MRI DB and the museum's object records.
  2. Why is this called Starchild 2? The name is borrowed from a more famous skull from Mexico.
  3. Why does the long head recur in Cairo's stone images? Amarna art and the Peruvian skulls side by side.

Along the way: the display case of three heads with preserved hair, the collection owner's account and what the studies say. All eleven photos with their observations are in the photo index at the end.

The skulls in the photos are human remains. This page tells only what can be seen in the photographs and exhibition labels and what published research says. A photograph cannot tell us the age, illnesses or origin of the deceased.

An old photograph, a new tracing question: where does MRI DB lead?

  1. What the photo shows. On the side of the right-hand skull, MRI DB and a string of numbers are handwritten. The number possibly looks like 3421, but the end is uncertain, so the reading is left open.
  2. Comparison with museum material. A Page 25 of García Soto's 2018 report shows an object record from the Ica regional museum. Under “Otros códigos e inscripciones” it reads MRI-DB-3869. The skull on the record is in the Museo Regional de Ica “Adolfo Bermúdez Jenkins”, its find site is recorded as Hacienda Chongos and its other code as MRI-01050-12 [2].
  3. Result. A match was found for the format of the code: the same MRI-DB prefix is used on the Ica museum's object records. An individual match remains open. The numbers do not match, and the prefix alone neither identifies the skull Jani photographed nor links it to the report's DNA results.
  4. Next question. Confirming the full number from a sharper photo, and the corresponding catalogue record from the museum.

Three places are kept apart. The report's skulls and object records come from the Ica regional museum [2], which is a different institution from the Navarro museum [3]. The display case of three heads with preserved hair and the collection owner's account are from the Navarro museum in Paracas (display case). The name of the back room is not shown in the video (back room). Where photo 08 was taken has not been confirmed, and identifying one place does not identify the whole material. A reverse image search with all of Jani's skull photos (20 November 2026) found the same display cases or labels nowhere else online. A traveller's TripAdvisor photo from the Navarro museum shows a glass-shelf display case of the same type as in photo 07, but the same skulls cannot be identified in it [20].

The display case of three heads with preserved hair

In Jani's video dNgc018HQNA three heads rest on separate glass stands. Dark hair-like material surrounds the side of the left-hand head. The middle one is an elongated pale skull with a dark area of hair on the crown. The right-hand dark head, smaller than the others, is on its own stand.

Page 175 of the Peruvian Ministry of Culture's 2023 museum guide shows three very similar-looking heads in the same order on glass stands. The video was filmed at the Museo Histórico Paracas “Juan Navarro Hierro” in Paracas. The guide presents the museum under this name, and it is run by the Navarro family [1]. Jani Korpi confirmed the filming location in 2026. The Ica regional museum is a different institution [3]. The guide does not confirm a family relationship, a shared grave or where the other photos were taken. The light and angle differ from the guide's photo.

The collection owner's account

L In the video dNgc018HQNA (filmed 1 November 2014) the narrator is the owner of the collection at the Museo Histórico Paracas “Juan Navarro Hierro”. According to him, the collection is national heritage but in his ownership. He says that one skull is not from the Americas and states that its DNA matches Northern Europe: Norway, Sweden, Finland or Russia. About the display case of three heads with preserved hair, he says that a father, a mother and a baby were found in the same grave, and that the father's and mother's hair was red and the baby's blond. In the video the owner does not show a research report, a laboratory or a sample code.

Is there a research code for the individual in the photo? None was found. No readable code is visible on the heads in the display case, and the museum guide does not mention a DNA study [1]. In García Soto's report, one skull from a looted grave at Cerro Colorado (S8) yielded mitochondrial group H, which according to the report is typical in Northern Europe. Mitochondrial DNA, however, traces only one maternal line, and haplogroup H alone does not place a person in Northern Europe: it is also common, for example, in North Africa (Ennafaa et al. 2009). A second sample from the same skull yielded group B5 [2]. The differing results for the same skull need explaining. The report is not peer-reviewed, no radiocarbon dates were obtained, and it does not demonstrate that the DNA is authentically ancient; modern contamination is a common explanation in such samples and has to be ruled out (Renaud et al. 2019). The report's skulls are in the storage of the Ica regional museum, and none of them can be linked to the heads in this display case.

The videos' question-style titles, such as “Ancient Skulls of Peru: Evidence of an Advanced Human Species?” (aqPy1X1mhGY) and “Ancient Skulls of humanoids: Proof of an Advanced Human Species?” (yIA-Dykw_40), are the names of the videos S . Published research does not support the idea of a separate human species: in the studied Paracas material the skulls belong to people whose heads were shaped in childhood [4].

Starchild 2 and the back room

L In the collection and in Jani's video Investigating the Starchild 2 skull of Peru (Nov 2014) the wide, round skull is called Starchild 2. The name is borrowed from another, more famous skull from Mexico. The two skulls are best kept apart:

Starchild 1 · Mexico

Source of the name
The skull later came to be called the Starchild skull [7]. It was made famous by the American author and researcher of paranormal phenomena Lloyd Pye (1946–2013), who brought the skull to public attention in the late 1990s and presented it for years as a human–alien hybrid [14]. Pye led the Starchild Project, which its own texts describe as an informal research group that has coordinated research since February 1999 [15]. Pye's official website lloydpye.com is still online [16].
Known origin
L According to the story, the skull was found in the early 1930s in the Chihuahua region of Mexico [7]. The find story is not a research finding.
L According to the project's own account, in the 1930s an American teenage girl found two skeletons in an abandoned mine tunnel in the Copper Canyon area and took two skulls home to El Paso. In 1998 the skulls were given to Ray and Melanie Young, who asked Pye to join. According to the project, radiocarbon dating gave the skull an age of about 900 years [15].
Research data linked to the individual
A An article in the magazine Skeptical Inquirer summarises two DNA tests from 1999 and 2003, according to which the skull is human [7]. For the 2003 analysis, a text copy published online was read. The signed original report and the authenticity of the copy have not been verified, so the next paragraph describes the content of that copy.
A Trace Genetics laboratory report of 12 August 2003, whose text has been published online as a copy of the project's material: the mitochondrial DNA of the Starchild skull sample (SCS-1) matched, in two separate extractions, Native American haplogroup C, and the sample of a second skull presented with it (SA-1) matched haplogroup A. According to the report, the different mtDNA types rule out a mother–child relationship, and the mothers of both were probably Native American. Nuclear DNA could not be amplified from the Starchild sample [17]. A signed original is not available to view.
L According to the project's own 2011 report, an unnamed geneticist sequenced four mtDNA fragments, and the project interpreted the differences from humans as exceptionally large. According to the report, some of the researchers remain anonymous for now [15]. The result has not been published in peer-reviewed form, so here it is a claim by the project.

Cairo 2024: similar long heads in Egypt

On 15 February 2024, at the Egyptian Museum in Cairo, Jani photographed stone heads whose crown extends far back. The shape resembles the Peruvian skulls, but the photos were taken on another continent and about nine years later than the Peruvian skull photos (2014).

Side by side: sculpture and human remains

What to look at: the curve of the crown, the line of the forehead, and how far the back of the head extends behind the ear. The photos were taken from different directions and distances, so exact proportions cannot be compared.

Is the long crown an artistic convention or a real head shape? A The object description of the Egyptian Museum in Berlin answers: according to it, the daughters' backward-elongated heads are a pure stylistic device and not a sign of hereditary or intentionally produced deformities [13]. A According to the Metropolitan Museum's exhibition publication, art historians have held that the Amarna artists exaggerated entirely ordinary physical features for stylistic reasons [19].

T Amarna-period art depicts the daughters of Pharaoh Akhenaten with elongated heads. According to the Global Egyptian Museum, the Cairo museum's quartzite head JE 44869 is probably Meritaten, Akhenaten's eldest daughter, and it was found in 1912 in the workshop of the sculptor Thutmose [11]. A princess head of the same type is in the Metropolitan Museum in New York [12]. According to the Cairo museum's guide, JE 44869 is 21 cm high and on display in room 3 on the ground floor [18], and another princess head from the same workshop, JE 44870 (yellow quartzite, 19 cm), is also in Cairo [19]. Jani's photo shows two heads side by side, but their correspondence to these two objects cannot be confirmed. The object numbers cannot be made out in Jani's photos, so the exact identification of the heads he filmed remains open.

Watch in the video: two stone heads, at 51:22 · museum tour from 51:31 (Travel South: Exploring the Egyptian Museum in Cairo, filmed 15 February 2024). The Peruvian counterpart: Investigating Elongated Skulls in a Peruvian Museum (filmed 1 November 2014).

The link to Peru is only visual. The Amarna heads are sculptures, the Peruvian skulls human remains; in the studied Paracas material the long head has been interpreted as shaping done in childhood (research background). The Cairo photos were taken in 2024, the Peruvian photos in 2014. AI illustrations of these pairs are on the stone city page: pair 12 and pair 13 (Cairo's stone faces and Peru's skulls).

S What would you set out to investigate?

The photos raise questions that many viewers ask. Here they are gathered as speculation, and for each one we say what kind of evidence would settle the question.

Are the elongated skulls another species?
It would take a sample code linked to the individual, a catalogue record, and a published analysis of the skeleton and genome compared with human material. In the studied Paracas material the skulls are human [4].
What would DNA tell us?
The first step is a sample code that links this particular skull to a laboratory report. Without it, the DNA result told in the video (the owner's account) remains a story.
Was there an advanced civilisation behind it?
It would take a find context, dating and artefacts, published in a verifiable way. An imagined version is opened up in the story Faces of the Stone City, and it is fiction.

What can be concluded from the studies?

TopicWhat the research saysSource
Cranial shaping in ParacasT Among the dead of Paracas Cavernas (Cerro Colorado, about 550–200 BCE), the skull shape of 159 individuals was classified. Shaping was observed in 98 per cent, and the most common type was vertical tabular shaping. The figure concerns the dead in this study, not the whole population.[4]
Shape of the palate and cranial vaultT 19 adult skulls from Peru in the collection of the Museum of Anthropology and Ethnology of the University of Florence, 9 shaped and 10 unshaped. The shape of the upper part of the skull differed between the groups; the average shape of the palate did not differ significantly. The study concerned the palate, not the appearance of the face, and the sample was small.[8]
DNA of the regionT 218 individuals from the Palpa area, the Paracas peninsula and the highlands, about 800 BCE–800 CE. A coding-region result was obtained from 130 individuals and a control-region sequence from 104. The Nasca population was genetically close to the preceding Paracas population. Population data do not tell the origin of the deceased in an individual photograph.[10]
The 2018 reportA García Soto's final report: eight skulls from the storage of the Ica regional museum, 30 samples, of which five produced a result. No radiocarbon dates were obtained. Results (table 3): B4 from two skulls, H and R from one, H and B5 from different samples of skull S8. In three samples the DNA was too degraded and one was not analysed. The author calls for further analyses. This is a report, not a peer-reviewed article. No link to the individuals in Jani's photos was found.[2]
Openings and a small skullT How an opening in the skull came about, the age of the deceased and the cause of the shape remain open from a photograph. An opening can be made by people through a surgical procedure, trepanation, but similar openings are also caused by congenital anomalies, injuries, infections, tumours and damage in the soil. Telling them apart requires examining the bone.[6]
The Starchild nameA The name comes from the skull of a reported find in the Chihuahua region of Mexico. According to the Skeptical Inquirer article, two DNA tests in 1999 and 2003 found it to be a human skull. For Peru's Starchild 2 no object record or study was found that would link that result to it.[7]

What would confirming the individual require? S The strongest candidate is the right-hand skull in photo 08 (tracing). A sharp close-up of the whole code or the museum's catalogue record could link the photograph to a find site and a sample code. Without this link, age, dating, kinship and DNA result remain open.

What is being researched on the topic in 2026?

Two studies published this year show where research on cranial shaping is heading.

  • TWard et al., iScience 2026. The researchers compiled a dataset of 1,772 cases of intentional cranial modification from the Americas and examined its temporal and geographical patterns. According to the modelling, the practice appeared first in Central America and then spread to North and South America. Annular shaping appeared in regions where tabular shaping already existed, and spread rapidly [9].
  • TOxilia et al., Applied Sciences 2026. A three-dimensional shape analysis of 19 Peruvian skulls (9 shaped, 10 unshaped): the shape of the braincase differed clearly between the groups, the shape of the palate did not. In these skulls, then, the shaping showed in the braincase but not in the average shape of the palate [8].

Neither study identifies the individuals Jani photographed or shows a link between Egypt and Peru. Nor does Oxilia's result confirm the faces of the illustrated figures on the stone city page; they are fiction.
