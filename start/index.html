Travel South: photographs and stories

A photograph from Peru, 2014: Jani Korpi is holding a wide, round skull that the collection calls Starchild 2. The name is borrowed from a different skull in Mexico, made known by Lloyd Pye; this gallery keeps the two apart.

The same journeys also left a trail of stone. In 2011 he photographed Peru and Bolivia in travel order, from the Nazca coast to the salt of Uyuni: desert, reed islands, the zigzag walls of Sacsayhuamán above Cusco, and the fitted stones of Tiwanaku and Puma Punku.

On 15 February 2024, in the Egyptian Museum in Cairo, he photographed stone heads whose profile can recall the elongated skulls of Peru. The likeness is visual only: the Cairo heads are sculptures, the Peruvian skulls are bone. In studied Paracas material, a long head is read as childhood modification, not as a result of DNA.

Cairo 2024: similar long heads in Egypt

On 15 February 2024, at the Egyptian Museum in Cairo, Jani photographed stone heads whose crown extends far back. The shape resembles the Peruvian skulls, but the photos were taken on another continent and about nine years later than the Peruvian skull photos (2014).

The link to Peru is only visual. The Amarna heads are sculptures, the Peruvian skulls human remains; in the studied Paracas material the long head has been interpreted as shaping done in childhood (research background). The Cairo photos were taken in 2024, the Peruvian photos in 2014. AI illustrations of these pairs are on the stone city page: pair 12 and pair 13 (Cairo's stone faces and Peru's skulls).

Who taught whom?

At Puma Punku a fingertip gives the scale of an inner corner carved into the stone. In Cusco the eye catches on a joint: it turns many times, and the edge of the next stone follows every turn. In 1549 Cieza de León heard at Tiwanaku that the Inca took these ruins as the model for the walls of Cusco. Sixty years later Garcilaso, of Inca descent, wrote the opposite: an Inca master built Tiwanaku. Radiocarbon places the building of Puma Punku at about AD 580–710, centuries before the Inca empire. Which story holds up when the stones are compared?

The stones do not support either story as it stands. The builders of Tiwanaku had been dead for centuries before the walls of Cusco, and the joints are made differently: at Tiwanaku the same standard part repeats and joints are locked with metal cramps, while in an Inca wall each stone is fitted to its neighbours. Even so, the joints alone cannot tell who learned from whom. The Inca knew the ruins, and Garcilaso's story shows that they wanted them as part of their own history. The same happens in Cusco: the Spaniards built on top of the Inca walls, and the 20th century repaired both.
