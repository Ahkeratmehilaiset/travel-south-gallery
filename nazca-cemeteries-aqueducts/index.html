Nazca Cemeteries & Aqueducts: Chauchilla, Cahuachi, Cantalloc

In the desert south of Nazca, the dead still sit in their tombs with their long braided hair intact.

At Chauchilla, most burials date from about AD 200 to 900, preserved by the bone-dry climate after looters scattered them for decades; Peru protected the site in 1997. Nearby, Cahuachi's adobe pyramids, used from about 100 BC to AD 500, are seen by excavators such as Giuseppe Orefici and Helaine Silverman as a pilgrimage centre. At Cantalloc, spiral shafts called puquios still feed water to fields.

But... how old are the puquios? Researchers usually attribute them to the Nazca around AD 500, while some archaeologists argue parts are colonial-era work; some enthusiasts ask whether they are far older than the Nazca.
