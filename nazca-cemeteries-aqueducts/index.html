Nazca Cemeteries & Aqueducts: Chauchilla, Cahuachi, Cantalloc

← Nazca LinesAll destinationsParacas & Huacachina →

In the desert south of Nazca, the dead still sit in their tombs with their long braided hair intact.

The tombs of Chauchilla hold burials from the Wari period to Inca times, about AD 700–1534. The tombs stretch over about two kilometres and are built of adobe (Peru tourism inventory). Nearby Cahuachi belongs to the Nasca culture, and many of its adobe pyramids were abandoned in the 4th century AD (Peru tourism inventory). Excavations have shown the centre's ceremonial role (Valdez 1994). At Cantalloc, spiral shafts called puquios still feed water to fields.

The age of the puquios is still debated.

On names: on this site Nasca means the ancient culture, and Nazca the present-day place names, such as the town and the lines.
